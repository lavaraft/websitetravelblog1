A Journey Across Asia

Asia has always felt impossibly big to me — a place of many worlds, languages, landscapes and stories. Traveling through it, country by country, made me realize just how different life can look and feel depending on where you stand. This website is where I’ve collected those experiences: the moments that shaped my time in China, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, and Japan.

From China’s mix of deep history and futuristic energy, to the endless skies and grounded traditions of Kazakhstan. From Vietnam’s chaotic, colorful streets that somehow still feel peaceful, to Japan’s quiet rituals hidden beneath neon lights — every country showed me a different side of Asia, and a different side of myself. None of these journeys felt the same, yet together they formed one larger story.

This homepage is meant to be the starting point of that story. A place where all the paths meet before they branch off into their own pages. If you want to know what surprised me, what challenged me, or what I’ll never forget from each country, you’ll find it in the sections beyond this page.

This isn’t a travel guide in the traditional sense. It’s more like a personal archive — a mix of impressions, thoughts, photos, and small details that stayed with me long after I left. I hope that somewhere in these memories, you find something that inspires you, whether it’s a new destination or simply a different way of looking at the world.

Welcome to my journey across Asia.

Travel Tips

System Fact Tips
Ride-Hailing Apps Apps like Grab (Southeast Asia) and DiDi (China) are dominant, providing safer, metered prices, and language barrier solutions compared to street taxis. Download and set up the relevant local app before you leave your home country. You need a local SIM card (or reliable data) to use them effectively.
Trains and Buses Rail systems range from the hyper-efficient (Japan, South Korea, China) to slower, older systems (Vietnam, Kazakhstan). Buses are often the cheapest long-distance option. Book long-distance tickets (especially sleeper trains) a few days in advance, particularly during holidays. For slower regions, be prepared for delays and crowded conditions.
Scooter/Motorbikes In Southeast Asian countries (Vietnam, Thailand), renting a scooter is common but carries significant risk and often invalidates travel insurance if you lack a local license. Be highly cautious. If renting, inspect the bike carefully and take photos of existing damage. Helmets are essential (and often required by law).
City Navigation Finding addresses can be difficult, as street numbering is not always sequential or logical. Download offline maps (like Google Maps) for the regions you visit. Use photos of landmarks or specific names of hotels/attractions to communicate your destination clearly.
